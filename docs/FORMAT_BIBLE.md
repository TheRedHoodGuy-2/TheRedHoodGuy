# FORMAT BIBLE: how a theredhoodguy storytime video is written, cut and shot

Status: DRAFT 2026-11-16, built from the Gneef study. Becomes law when Xavier signs it off.
Source data: 75 Gneef Shorts (the 63 at 10M+ views and the 12 newest). All 75 transcribed with word timings and cut
detection; camera motion measured on 31 so far; 2 studied shot by shot on contact sheets. Numbers below are medians of the
51 winners longer than 90 s unless it says otherwise. Raw numbers: `reference/gneef/stats.json`.
We copy the FORMULA. We never reuse their premises, scripts, character names or shots.

## 1. The premise (the whole video lives or dies here)
- One impossible rule for a whole town or world, sayable in one breath. 47 of 51 open with "In this world / town / city".
- The rule must be VISUAL (a number on the chest, a price tag over the head, a string on the back, a magnet colour on the
  wrist) and must create daily-life comedy AND a way to get hurt.
- Families of rules that win: a body counter or tag everyone wears; a "never do X or else" law (19 of 51); a thing that
  comes at night (12 of 51); powers with a catch (7 of 51).
- Title = the rule itself in about 5 words + one emoji (62 of 63 end on an emoji). No clickbait wording, no "POV".
- Test before writing: can a 10-year-old repeat the rule after hearing it once, and can they already imagine one funny
  consequence and one scary one? If not, the premise is not ready.

## 2. Script skeleton (beats, with where they land in the runtime)
1. THE RULE, line 1, done by 3.0 s. Voice starts at 0.0 s. No greeting, no intro.
2. "You see, ..." how the rule works, 1 to 2 sentences (51 of 54 use these exact two words). Done by about 9 s.
3. LIFE WITH THE RULE: 3 to 5 one-line gags, each one its own shot (wagons to school, zip-line grandmas, pogo mailman).
   Ends around 18 to 20% of the runtime.
4. "BUT I ..." the narrator's own problem under this rule (median at 27%). First person, a kid (45 of 54 first person).
   Something they lack or are ashamed of. Name 1 to 2 people they love (44 of 51 end on mom / dad / sibling / grandma).
5. THE PLANT: one small odd habit or skill, shown as a throwaway (handstands every night). It is the key to the ending.
6. "UNTIL ONE ..." the crisis, at 28 to 39% (median 34%). Someone the narrator loves is in danger because of the rule.
7. ESCALATION with a clock: countdown numbers, days of the week, a chain forming. Short sentences, a cut on every one.
8. THE WALL: the obvious solution fails or the cost lands ("My legs turned to stone").
9. "AND THAT'S WHEN I REALIZED" (median at 56%): the plant + a loophole in the exact wording of the rule.
10. PAYOFF: the save, then a quiet human beat (a hug, a look).
11. THE WORLD CHANGED: one or two lines showing the town adopting it, or a last small joke. Last word lands 0.3 s
    before the video ends. Only 9 of 54 add a "subscribe" line, and when they do it is a one-line joke in character.

## 3. Sentence and word rules
- Length: winners run 140 s / 598 words. The 12 winners under 90 s run 40 s / 173 words and still got 10M to 19M.
  Longer correlates mildly with more views (r = 0.35).
- OUR TARGET for the first videos: 75 to 95 s, 320 to 410 words. Reason: we render about 7 s per frame, so 85 s is about
  4 hours; 140 s is over 6. Lengthen once the pipeline holds.
- Pace: 262 words per minute, no gaps (2 pauses over 0.45 s in a whole video; longest pause 0.5 s).
- Sentences: median 6 words. 41% are 5 words or fewer. Only 2% reach 20 words. Fragments are welcome ("Alone." "Footsteps.").
- Past tense, plain words a child knows, concrete nouns. Numbers said out loud as they change.
- Dialogue: 3 to 4 short spoken lines per video, each tagged with a verb (said, whispered, screamed).
- 2 named characters besides the narrator. Simple first names (Theo, Rosie, Lily).
- Every sentence must be drawable as ONE picture. If it cannot be shown, cut it or rewrite it.

## 4. Cutting
- 81 shots per video, one cut every 1.46 s (median); average 1.7 s. Newest uploads cut faster: 1.1 s median, 108 shots.
- One sentence or clause = one shot = one picture. About 7.7 words per shot.
- First shot lasts 1.9 s. 7 cuts happen inside the first 10 s. Longest shot in a whole video: about 6 s, saved for the
  climax or the emotional beat.
- Hard cuts only. No fades, no wipes.

## 5. Camera
- Almost never still: of 267 measured shots, 72% slowly push in, 22% pull out, 5% drift sideways, 1% are locked off.
  Typical push: the picture grows about 7% per second.
- One subject per shot, centred, medium to medium-close (head to knees), camera slightly below eye level looking up a
  little. Wide shots only to show a place or a crowd, then straight back in.
- The subject sits in the upper two thirds; the caption owns the band just below the middle.
- Inserts carry the rule: the number on the chest, the sign, the tag, the hand on the handle. They get their own shot.

## 6. Captions and on-screen text
- One word at a time, the word being spoken. White, heavy rounded sans, black outline, slightly below centre
  (about 72% down the frame). No colours, no emoji, no boxes.
- Rule props are real objects in the world (chest counters, price-tag bubbles, signs), not captions.

## 7. Characters and world (what the reference actually looks like)
- Plain Roblox R6 bodies, flat skin colours, classic 2D shirt / pants, catalog hair, simple decal faces that change
  with the emotion in nearly every shot. Nothing fancier than that.
- The narrator is a recurring, instantly readable kid (strong hair colour). Family and extras each read by one bold
  colour or hair shape.
- A realistic, reusable town: suburban street, houses with porches, park with a hill, school, shop, a few interiors.
  Blue sky, hard sun, clean shadows, grass and trees with real texture. 8 to 10 locations per long video.
- Crowds matter: classes, neighbours, a whole park. Extras are cheap variations of the same body.

## 8. Checklist for a script before it goes to Xavier
[ ] Rule in line 1, under 3 s. [ ] "You see," in sentence 2 or 3. [ ] 3+ life-with-the-rule gags by 20%.
[ ] "But I" by 30%. [ ] The plant is shown before the crisis. [ ] Crisis by 40%, with a clock.
[ ] The fix uses the plant AND the rule's own wording. [ ] Ends on a loved one, then the world changes.
[ ] Median sentence 6 words; no sentence over 20. [ ] Every sentence is one drawable shot. [ ] 320 to 410 words.

## Comment bait (2026-11-25)
- COMMENT BAIT (Xavier 2026-11-25 08:44, from Tag Swap's comments: "cheeky boy" because he laughed in the last frame
  while his bro became a girl; people debating "what if you were trans / a boy turning into a girl"): every script must
  give people something to TALK about: a debatable rule or a "what would YOU do" choice, a character moment viewers
  have an opinion on (cheeky, unfair, wholesome, sus), a last frame that invites a reaction, an open question in the
  ending. Before approval, list the 2-3 comments the script is designed to start.
