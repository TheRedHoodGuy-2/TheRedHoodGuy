# The science behind each video

## Head rush / low iron (2026-10-23)
- Standing up fast pools blood in the legs; the blood pressure reflex takes a few seconds to catch up, so the brain is
  briefly short of blood. Low iron = less oxygen carried, so it hits harder.
- Sources: [StatPearls: orthostatic hypotension](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK448192/),
  [Merck Manual](https://www.merck.com/mmhe/sec03/ch023/ch023c.html),
  [SiPhox Health](https://siphoxhealth.com/articles/why-do-i-feel-dizzy-only-when-standing-up)

## Stomach growl (2026-10-24)
- An empty stomach triggers a wave of muscle squeezes (the migrating motor complex) about every couple of hours to sweep
  leftovers; with no food to muffle it, gas and air make the noise (borborygmi).
- Sources: [Scientific American](https://scientificamerican.com/article/why-does-your-stomach-gro/),
  [Acibadem: borborygmi](https://acibademinternational.com/blog/why-is-my-stomach-making-noise-borborygmi-explained/)

## Hypnic jerk (2026-11-11)
- A sudden involuntary twitch while falling asleep, often with a falling feeling. Leading idea: as muscles relax at sleep
  onset the brain misreads it as falling and fires the muscles. Common and harmless.
- Script line used: "your muscles relax so fast your brain panics. It thinks you're falling, so it slams every muscle".
