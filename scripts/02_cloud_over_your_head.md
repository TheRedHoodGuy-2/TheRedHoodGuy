# Everyone Has A Cloud Over Their Head 🌧️

STATUS: draft for Xavier. Not approved.
Cast: narrator (red hood kid), little sister Zoe, pony Biscuit, Dad, teacher, mayor, firefighters, fair crowd.
Places: street, classroom, garden, bus, lunch hall, summer fair at night, old barn.
Rule props: a small cloud over every head (sun, lightning or rain), dry hose, hot dog cart.
Plant: he learned a thousand jokes because a good joke stops his rain for a second. Loophole: clouds rain when you CRY, not when you are sad.

## Script
In this town, everyone is born with a tiny cloud over their head. You see, your cloud shows exactly how you feel. Happy people get sunshine. Angry people get lightning. And sad people get rain. So nobody here can hide a bad day. My teacher zaps the chalkboard twice a week. My dad waters the garden by watching sad movies. And you never, ever sit next to a baby on the bus.

But my cloud is broken. It has rained on me every single day of my life. I'm not even sad. It just rains. So nobody sits with me at lunch. They call me Puddles. The only thing that ever worked was jokes. If I tell one good enough, my rain stops, for about a second. So I learned a thousand of them.

Until one summer, the big fair came to town. For thirty days, nobody had one sad thought. Not one drop of rain. The grass turned yellow. The river dried up.

Then, on the last night, a firework landed on the old barn. My little sister Zoe's pony, Biscuit, was inside. And Zoe ran in after him.

The firefighters dragged the hose to the river. Nothing came out. Cry, the mayor shouted. Everybody, cry! People tried. They squeezed their eyes shut. They thought about homework. One guy pinched himself. But you can't be sad on purpose. A few drops fell. The fire got bigger.

I stood by the barn with my one little rain cloud. It wasn't enough.

And that's when I realized something. Clouds don't rain when you're sad. They rain when you cry. So I climbed on top of the hot dog cart, and I yelled the best joke I had. Why did the scarecrow win a prize? Nobody laughed. So I told another one. And another.

Then my dad snorted. Then the mayor. Then one lady laughed so hard, she fell over. Three hundred people, laughing until tears ran down their faces. And three hundred clouds burst open at the same time.

It poured. The fire hissed, and went out. Zoe walked out of the smoke holding Biscuit's rope, soaking wet, and still giggling.

Nobody calls me Puddles anymore. Now, when the fields get dry, the farmers knock on my door and ask for the one about the scarecrow. My cloud still rains every day. But I'm never under it alone.
