# In This Town, Gravity Turns Off At Noon 🎈

STATUS: draft for Xavier. Not approved.
Cast: narrator (red hood kid), Grandpa (clock keeper), Mom, baby Ollie, Dad, baker, mailman, class (crowd).
Places: market square with clock tower, clock room, kitchen, classroom, sky above the roofs.
Rule props: ropes on everyone at noon, town clock face with a brass minute hand and one loose bolt, stroller.
Plant: the loose bolt on the minute hand. Loophole: gravity comes back when the CLOCK says 12:01.

## Script
In this town, gravity turns off for one minute, every day, at noon. You see, the second the town clock strikes twelve, everything that isn't tied down floats. So at 11:59, everybody grabs a rope. Dogs go up like balloons. Teachers staple the homework to the desks. And nobody, ever, orders soup at noon. My dad learned that the hard way.

But I hate noon. Every other kid lets go of their rope and does flips. Not me. I get dizzy on a swing. So every day at noon, I hide in the clock tower with my grandpa. He's the clock keeper. He lets me oil the gears. He even showed me the big brass minute hand, and the one loose bolt that holds it. Don't ever touch that, he said.

Until one Saturday, my mom took me and my baby brother Ollie to the market. It was 11:59. Mom tied my rope. She tied her rope. Then the bell rang. Twelve. And Ollie's stroller floated right past us. Nobody had tied it down.

Mom screamed and jumped, but her rope snapped tight. The stroller kept rising. Past the rooftops. Past the birds. And in sixty seconds, gravity was coming back. Fifty. Everybody stared up. Nobody let go of their rope. Forty. So I untied mine.

My stomach flipped. I floated up, past the bakery, past the flagpole. Thirty. I reached for the stroller. Too far. Twenty. I was never going to make it.

And that's when I looked at the clock tower. Gravity doesn't come back after a minute. It comes back when the clock says 12:01. So I kicked off the flagpole, flew straight at the clock, and grabbed the minute hand. Ten. Five. I pulled the loose bolt. Three. Two. One.

Nothing happened. It was still noon.

Grandpa stuck his head out of the tower. What did you do? Then he saw Ollie, and he threw me every rope he had. The baker floated up. Then the mailman. Then my whole class, one long chain, all the way to the stroller. They passed Ollie down, hand to hand, right into my mom's arms. Then they pulled each other back to the ground, and I let the minute hand go.

I still get dizzy on swings. But now, every year on Ollie's birthday, the whole town floats up. And my grandpa lets me hold noon for five extra minutes.
