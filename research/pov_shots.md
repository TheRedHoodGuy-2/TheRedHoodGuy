# POV shots that actually read as POV (2026-11-28, for New Body; sources: Animation Mentor POV tutorial, StudioBinder, Soundstripe, Descript)
1. ORIENT FIRST: cut to the POV from an objective shot of the character, so the audience knows whose eyes they are in (Descript rule #1). For a body-swap: show the body, then cut inside it.
2. EYE LEVEL, HEAD LOGIC: camera exactly at the character's eye height and pitch; it moves like a HEAD (turn, settle, small corrections), never like a drone (Animation Mentor: too high/low stops feeling like their view).
3. HANDS + BOB ARE THE ILLUSION: the viewer's own hands enter the frame LARGE and close to the lens, and the camera bobs/sways with breath and steps. "Drop either and it turns into a floating drone shot" (kling guide). 
4. WIDE LENS: POVs are shot wide (even fisheye) so near hands and the world both read (Soundstripe).
5. HANDHELD URGENCY: a light handheld layer sells subjectivity (StudioBinder/captions.ai).
6. BODY-SWAP EXTRA: tilt DOWN at the new body (torso, legs, shoes from above) = the instant "I am in someone else's body" read; games do this on possession/respawn.
Recipe for ours: objective shot of the body -> cut in at her eye height -> look at the yeller -> TILT DOWN own torso/legs -> hands rise close to lens (big, fov ~60) -> turn palms -> tilt back up; breath bob + micro-corrections the whole time.
