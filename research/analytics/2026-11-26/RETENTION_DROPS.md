# Where our viewers leave (pulled 2026-11-26 via Windsor: YouTube audience retention curves + TikTok per-second retention)
Raw: yt_retention_curves.json. TikTok numbers below (per-second % still watching).

## Headline
- Storytime crushes explainers. TikTok Gender Swap (61.7 s): 22.2k views, 48% watched to the end, avg 39 s, 124 comments,
  130 shares. The explainers on TikTok: 620-700 views, 15-24% full watch. (Storytime is not on YouTube yet.)
- YouTube explainers, relative retention vs similar-length videos: jolts .82, gaming .69, forget-room .54, brain freeze .50,
  comeback .48, tickle .31.

## Drop pattern 1: EXPOSITION is the biggest mid-video leak
- Gender Swap 3-9 s ("You see, boys turn into girls... lasts until the next sunset. So at 5:59"): 2.4-2.7 %/s lost, the worst
  stretch after the hook. The gags right after (9-33 s): 1.0-1.3 %/s.
- Brain freeze 12-18 s ("your brain panics at the cold and sets off every alarm..."): 5.4-5.7 %/s, its worst stretch.
- Tickle 15-21 s (the brain-prediction explanation): 3.8 / 2.9 %/s after it had settled to 1.3.
## Drop pattern 2: VAGUE / WITHHELD lines leak
- Gender Swap 33-36 s ("So this summer, I practiced one thing. Over and over. Then Friday"): spike to 2.1 %/s. Nothing to
  picture, and the secret is withheld with no image.
## Drop pattern 3: SLOW / PASSIVE openers leak at 0-3 s
- "He got bored in class so he tried..." (tickle) 9.6 %/s, gaming 8.8, jolts 8.3; question or action openers ("Why did he
  come in here?" doorway, "He snuck a slushie into class") 2.6-4.4 %/s.
## Hold pattern: a CLOCK on screen
- Gender Swap 39-54 s countdown ("Ten seconds... Five seconds... Two seconds"): 0.78-0.88 %/s, the best stretch of any video.
- Jolts / gaming payoff stretches (action toward the punchline): 0.8-1.3 %/s.
## Ending
- Everyone leaks after the payoff line (Gender Swap 57 s "What did you do?" 1.8 %/s; forget-room end -0.51 ratio). Cut right
  on the payoff; loop the last shot into the first.

## Rules this gives us (into every script)
1. The rule / "You see" exposition gets ONE short sentence, said over a gag that SHOWS it, never two explaining sentences.
2. Never withhold with a vague line ("I practiced one thing"); show something we can picture that raises a question.
3. Open on action or a question (or the cold-open climax), never on a passive setup.
4. Put a visible clock on the crisis and tick it often (days, seconds): it is our lowest-loss device.
5. Cut immediately after the payoff line.
