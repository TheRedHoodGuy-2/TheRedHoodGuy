# Retention: composing, editing, leverage (Xavier's reference video, 2026-11-26)
Source: an 11-min YouTube tutorial on short-form retention Xavier sent (audio transcribed; frames skipped per his note).
Core frame: retention graph as flat as possible. Car analogy: COMPOSING = engine, EDITING = body, LEVERAGE = jet booster.
## Composing (hook -> storytelling -> payoff)
- Hook < 5 s: concise, instantly understood, states the idea AND promises a payoff. Visuals can carry the idea; on-screen
  text alone can be the hook (a caption that makes you want to see the thing).
- Storytelling: conflict (things go wrong, then resolve), suspense words (but, however), and above all TENSION =
  anticipation framed toward the payoff (even a low-stakes premise holds if every beat builds toward it).
- Payoff must meet the promise set in the hook; a let-down ending ("it's a tie") costs returning viewers, who are the real
  retention engine.
## Editing
- Pacing as fast as possible: condense to the most action-packed moments (shoot / render far more than you keep).
- Captions / transitions / zooms: copy what works in your own niche.
- Music per section changes mood; a build-up track manufactures tension; wrong track backfires. SFX where the style fits.
## Leverage
- Engagement bait: an intentional mistake or a crazy moment people need to point out; time in the comments keeps the short
  playing, so comments raise retention.
- Seeded comment from another account ("that ending was crazy") makes people wait for the end (DECEPTIVE: we do not do this;
  pin a real viewer comment instead).
- Related-video link to another of our shorts.
## For us
- Our title layer = the text hook; Title must promise the payoff (e.g. the rule + what goes wrong).
- Every script: name the promise in the hook and check the last shot pays it off.
- Music: one track per section (calm gag music -> build-up under the crisis) instead of one loop.
- Plant one deliberate "mistake" / detail viewers will comment on (we already do the plant; make one of them pointable).
